# 06 — Desire Dimensions: A Consolidated Model for B2B Buyer Motivation

> **Purpose:** A comprehensive, agent-usable desire-dimension model for auto-gtme's L3 (ICP-why layer). Every dimension includes: what it captures, one probing question the agent answers via research, a concrete example for **ICP-A** (developer-founder who wants to build product, not do outreach) and **ICP-B** (VC firm losing dealflow to competitors).

---

## Part 1: Framework Survey

### 1. Bain & Company — B2B Elements of Value (40 Elements)

**Source:** [The B2B Elements of Value — HBR, 2018](https://hbr.org/2018/03/the-b2b-elements-of-value) | [Interactive Pyramid](https://media.bain.com/b2b-eov/)

Bain organized 40 elements into a five-level pyramid rooted in Maslow's hierarchy. Based on 30+ years of quantitative client research. Key finding: vendors delivering 4+ elements score 3.5x higher NPS than single-element vendors. The individual and inspirational layers (career risk, anxiety reduction, vision) drive loyalty more than functional or economic layers — yet most B2B messaging never reaches them.

**The five levels:**

| Level | Elements (abbreviated) |
|---|---|
| **Table Stakes** | Meet specs, acceptable price, regulatory compliance, ethical standards |
| **Functional** | Cost reduction, revenue growth, product quality, scalability, innovation |
| **Ease of Doing Business** | Time savings, reduced effort, simplification, integration, transparency, responsiveness, expertise, stability, cultural fit, flexibility, risk reduction |
| **Individual** | Reduced anxiety, reputational assurance, network expansion, marketability, growth/development, design/aesthetics, fun & perks |
| **Inspirational** | Vision, hope for the future, social responsibility |

**Agent takeaway:** Most outbound copy lands at Functional. The winning messages address Individual (anxiety, reputation, career growth) and Inspirational (vision, hope). Map every prospect to which pyramid level they currently experience their pain at.

### 2. Jobs To Be Done (JTBD) — Christensen / Moesta

**Source:** [Christensen Institute — JTBD Theory](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) | [Four Forces Diagram — Bob Moesta, JTBD.info](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/unpacking-the-progress-making-forces-diagram/) | [GoPractice synthesis](https://gopractice.io/product/jobs-to-be-done-the-theory-and-the-frameworks/)

People don't buy products — they hire solutions to make progress on a job. Every JTBD has three layers:

- **Functional job** — the concrete task: "get more qualified pipeline without spending my time on cold outreach"
- **Emotional job** — how they want to feel: "feel like a founder who ships, not a SDR"
- **Social job** — how they want to be seen: "look like someone who has distribution figured out"

**The Four Forces of Progress** (Bob Moesta) explain the switch decision:

| Force | Direction | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Push** | → switch | Dissatisfaction with current situation building until it becomes untenable |
| **Pull** | → switch | Attraction toward a concrete image of a better future state |
| **Anxiety** | ← stay | Uncertainty about whether the new thing will actually work, fear of switching cost |
| **Inertia/Habit** | ← stay | Comfort with current process even when rationally suboptimal |

**Switch rule:** Push + Pull must outweigh Anxiety + Inertia. Outbound copy that only amplifies Pull without addressing Anxiety and Inertia loses to the status quo.

**Agent takeaway:** For every prospect, map which force is dominant. Developer founders are usually high-inertia (they'd rather write code than evaluate tools) and high-anxiety (new tools = new failure modes). The right message raises Push (time being wasted) and lowers Anxiety (this runs itself).

### 3. Harvard — "The New Science of Customer Emotions" (Magids, Zorfas, Leemon 2015)

**Source:** [HBR Article — November 2015](https://hbr.org/2015/11/the-new-science-of-customer-emotions)

Motista + CEB analyzed 300 emotional motivators across hundreds of brands and industries. Identified 25 high-significance motivators; the most commercially powerful (driving 2x spend and 2x purchase frequency) cluster into three B2B-relevant categories:

**Security cluster:**
- Feel secure in a purchase decision (reduce risk of being wrong)
- Have confidence this is the popular/defensible choice
- Confidence this is right for the future

**Status & distinction cluster:**
- Stand out from the crowd / be seen as ahead of the curve
- Feel a sense of thrill from making a bold, forward-looking choice
- Project competence and confidence to peers

**Belonging & alignment cluster:**
- Feel a sense of belonging to a tribe of successful peers
- Align with values and mission of the vendor

**Agent takeaway:** Most B2B outbound messages address functional needs. The buyers who convert at 2x rate are those who feel emotionally connected — specifically around security (was this the right call?) and distinction (am I ahead of the curve?). Messages that make the buyer feel *smart and safe* — simultaneously — outperform either alone.

### 4. Adele Revella — 5 Rings of Buying Insight (Buyer Persona Institute)

**Source:** [Buyer Persona Institute](https://blindspots.klue.com/episode/how-to-build-better-buyer-personas-with-adele-revellas-5-step-framework) | [Jeff Korhan summary](https://www.jeffkorhan.com/tag/5-rings-of-buying-insight) | [Medium book summary](https://medium.com/@leonie_nedden/buyer-personas-a-summary-of-insights-from-the-book-of-adelle-revella-3679b547a185)

Revella's framework captures the *decision psychology* around a specific buying event — not demographic profiles. Five rings:

| Ring | What it captures | Key question answered |
|---|---|---|
| **Priority Initiative** | Why are they looking NOW — the triggering circumstance | "What changed that made the status quo unacceptable?" |
| **Success Factors** | The operational and personal outcomes they expect | "What does 'winning' look like to them — and to their career?" |
| **Perceived Barriers** | What's blocking them from buying or from buying *you* | "Why would they say no, or choose a competitor?" |
| **Buyer's Journey** | Who is involved, where they research, what they evaluate | "Where are they in the decision and who else has a vote?" |
| **Decision Criteria** | The specific attributes they compare when choosing between finalists | "What are the 3 things they will defend to their CFO?" |

**Agent takeaway:** Revella's rings are a research checklist. Before writing a word of copy, an agent should have an answer to all five rings for the specific prospect. Priority Initiative (the trigger) and Perceived Barriers (anxiety) are the highest-leverage copy levers.

### 5. SCARF Model — David Rock / NeuroLeadership Institute (2008, updated 2025)

**Source:** [SCARF Model — Neuroleadership.com, 2025 update](https://www.neuroleadership.com/articles/the-evolution-of-the-social-brain-introducing-scarf-in-2025) | [Toolshero overview](https://www.toolshero.com/management/scarf-model-david-rock/)

Neuroscience-grounded model of five social domains that trigger approach/avoid responses in the brain — identical to physical survival responses. Each domain can be activated as a reward or threatened as a danger.

| Domain | Description | 2025 Priority Shift |
|---|---|---|
| **Status** | Relative standing, feeling respected for competence (not just title) | High — now tied to skill recognition not hierarchy |
| **Certainty** | Ability to predict the future, clear expectations | Dropped — became a hygiene factor post-pandemic |
| **Autonomy** | Sense of personal choice and agency | Highest — jumped dramatically 2012→2025 |
| **Relatedness** | Safety and belonging with a group | Moderate |
| **Fairness** | Perception of just, transparent exchange | Now #1 driver — activates same reward circuits as money |

**Agent takeaway:** The 2025 update is significant. Autonomy is now the top threat/reward lever in B2B — any messaging that feels like it removes control will trigger avoidance. Fairness (transparent pricing, honest tradeoffs) now activates reward circuits. For developer founders: autonomy is sacred. For VCs: status (being recognized as a top-tier firm) is the primary SCARF driver.

### 6. Apollo.io — Buyer Psychology Research

**Source:** [Psychology of Sales — Apollo.io](https://www.apollo.io/insights/psychology-of-sales) | [B2B Buyer Journey 2026 — Apollo.io](https://www.apollo.io/insights/b2b-buyer-journey)

Apollo's practitioner research surfaces four modern B2B psychology mechanisms:

1. **Category Anchoring:** Buyers form a mental model of solution types before any vendor contact. The first credible framing wins.
2. **Social Proof Hierarchy:** Peer reviews > analyst validation > vendor claims. "Who else like me uses this?"
3. **Consistency Verification:** Misalignment across channels eliminates vendors. Trust = coherence.
4. **ROI Defensibility:** Buyers need ammunition to justify internally. They are buying a *decision they can defend*, not just a product.

Additional finding: B2B buyers increasingly prefer a rep-free experience — [Gartner's March 2026 sales survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) puts it at 67% across all purchase complexities (the "75%" figure that circulates is Gartner's older 2019 result, which applied only to low-complexity purchases). The "89% use generative AI as a primary research method" figure is Apollo's claim in the B2B Buyer Journey report linked above; no independent primary source located. Either way the implication holds: copy must work on its own — without a sales rep to recover from a weak first message.

**Agent takeaway:** Design messages so buyers can self-justify. Give them the language to explain the decision to their CFO, their board, or their co-founder. The message is also a brief for the internal champion.

### 7. Challenger Sale / Insight Selling — Dixon & Adamson

**Source:** [Challenger Inc — What is Challenger Sales](https://challengerinc.com/what-is-challenger-sales-methodology/) | [Challenger Customer Profiles](https://challengerinc.com/the-challenger-customer-profiles/)

Key insight for desire-dimension modeling: buyers don't just want to solve a problem they already know about — they can be taught to perceive a problem they didn't previously frame as urgent. Commercial insight reframes the buyer's understanding of their own situation. This implies a dimension the other frameworks underweight: **Insight Value** — the desire to receive a perspective that makes you smarter about your own business.

Supporting data: CEB's stakeholder research (published in *The Challenger Customer*, Adamson, Dixon, Spenner & Toman, 2015; survey of 3,000+ B2B stakeholders) found purchase likelihood is 81% with a single decision-maker and collapses to 31% when six buyers are involved — the 81% figure and the "likelihood falls dramatically past five buyers" finding are restated on [Challenger's own blog](https://challengerinc.com/blog/more-b2b-decision-makers-want-in/). The "99% of B2B purchases are driven by organizational changes" line is widely attributed to CEB/Gartner but no primary publication was located — treat it as a directional claim (purchases follow trigger events), not a measured statistic. The biggest competitor in most B2B deals is the status quo, not another vendor.

### 8. B2B Buyer Fear / Identity / Career Risk Research

**Source:** [From Promotion to Emotion — CEB/Google/Motista (primary study, PDF)](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/131/promotion-emotion-b2b_articles.pdf) | [Hidden Consumer in B2B — McClellan, Medium 2026](https://medium.com/@rwmcclellan/the-hidden-consumer-in-b2b-why-individual-psychology-drives-business-decisions-f7d6fbabb218) | [Fear in B2B — The Marketing Meetup](https://themarketingmeetup.com/blog/fear-in-b2b/) | [B2B Buyer Psychology — IntentAmplify](https://intentamplify.com/blog/b2b-buyers-decisions-psychology/)

The strongest primary research here is the CEB/Google/Motista study [*From Promotion to Emotion*](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/131/promotion-emotion-b2b_articles.pdf) (survey of 3,000 B2B buyers across 36 brands): B2B buyers are *more* emotionally connected to vendors than B2C consumers (7 of 9 B2B brands studied had emotional connections with 50%+ of buyers, vs. 10–40% typical for B2C), and personal value had roughly 2× the impact of business value on willingness to buy, pay a premium, and advocate. (The "80% of B2B purchase decisions are influenced by emotional factors — Gartner 2025" figure that circulates in aggregator blogs has no locatable Gartner primary source; the CEB/Google/Motista findings are the verifiable version of the claim.) The primary emotion in B2B is **fear** — specifically:

- Fear of being wrong (making a career-damaging call)
- Fear of looking incompetent to peers or leadership
- Fear of implementation failure
- Fear of the unknown (will this vendor still exist in 2 years?)

Identity dimension: buyers ask *"What does this choice say about me as a professional?"* This is distinct from career risk — it's about professional self-image and the tribe signal sent by vendor selection.

## Part 2: Gap Analysis of Current 7-Dimension Model

The original auto-gtme model used: emotional, logical, factual, identity/belief, psychological, social, temporal.

| Original Dimension | Coverage vs. Research |
|---|---|
| Emotional | Partially covered — too generic; misses specific fear/security/distinction clusters |
| Logical | Covered by Functional/Table Stakes — but misses ROI defensibility and insight value |
| Factual | Weak — more a *channel* than a *dimension*; replace with Trigger/Push |
| Identity/belief | Solid — aligns with professional identity, tribal signal, JTBD social job |
| Psychological | Vague — replace with Fear/Risk and SCARF threat/reward |
| Social | Partially covered — misses peer-validation hierarchy and committee dynamics |
| Temporal | Covered — trigger events, compelling events |

**Key gaps:** No career-risk dimension. No autonomy/control dimension. No insight value. No barrier/inertia dimension. No ROI-defensibility dimension. Missing the distinction between org-level pain and individual-level fear.

## Part 3: Consolidated Desire-Dimension Model

> **Usage:** For each prospect, the agent runs through all 11 dimensions, answering the probing question from public signals (LinkedIn, blog posts, job postings, press releases, trigger events). The answer populates the ICP-why layer and drives message selection.

---

### D1 — Trigger Pain (Push)
**What it captures:** The specific circumstance that broke the status quo — the event that made inaction more expensive than action.

**Probing question:** "What changed in the last 60–90 days at this company or in this person's role that created urgency?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| Just hit a go-to-market wall: first 10 users came from the network, next 100 won't. Product is ready but the founder has no pipeline and no time to build one. | Missed a Series A deal to a competitor firm that had a warm intro. Deal flow this quarter is 30% below target; partners are asking why. |

---

### D2 — Functional Job (What they need to get done)
**What it captures:** The concrete, operational outcome they need — the task the solution must complete for them.

**Probing question:** "What specific operational output would a solution need to produce for this buyer to call it successful?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| Generate qualified sales meetings without the founder spending more than 2–3 hours per week on outreach. | Source 5–10 proprietary (non-intermediated) deals per month from target sectors before competitors see them. |

---

### D3 — Emotional Job (How they want to feel)
**What it captures:** The internal emotional state the buyer is moving toward — what relief, pride, or confidence the purchase provides.

**Probing question:** "What emotion is this person trying to achieve or avoid in their role right now?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| Wants to feel like a builder, not a salesperson. The emotional win is waking up to meetings already booked — without having spent Sunday writing cold emails. | Wants to feel like a market-maker, not a deal-taker. Relief from being reactive; confidence of being the firm founders call first. |

---

### D4 — Social/Identity Job (How they want to be seen)
**What it captures:** The professional identity signal the buyer sends to peers, leadership, or their market by making this purchase.

**Probing question:** "What tribe does this person belong to, and what would a smart member of that tribe do in this situation?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| Belongs to the "technical founder who figured out distribution" tribe — the YC companies that got to $1M ARR while staying engineering-led. Vendor choice signals "I'm sharp enough to automate GTM." | Belongs to the "differentiated specialist VC" tribe — known for access, not capital. Vendor choice signals "we invest in proprietary sourcing infrastructure, not just hustle." |

---

### D5 — Career / Reputational Risk (Fear of Being Wrong)
**What it captures:** The personal downside the buyer is protecting against — career damage, embarrassment, or the cost of a bad call.

**Probing question:** "What is the worst professional outcome for this person if the purchase goes wrong or produces no results?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| Burned $2–3K/month on a tool, wasted two months, and is still in the same place. Co-founder and investors see "founder can't figure out GTM" written on the wall. | Paid for a sourcing platform that produced noise, not signal. Partners lose confidence in the sourcing partner's judgment; deal thesis looks weak to LPs at next update. |

---

### D6 — Autonomy / Control (SCARF: Autonomy)
**What it captures:** The buyer's need to remain in control of their process, timeline, and decisions — especially sensitive in technical buyers.

**Probing question:** "Does this buyer need to feel like they own the process, or are they comfortable delegating it entirely?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| High autonomy need. Wants to see exactly what messages go out, be able to turn it off instantly, and never have a prospect receive something they'd be embarrassed by. "Black box" is an immediate veto. | Moderate autonomy need. Partners want input on targeting criteria and message positioning but don't want to manage day-to-day execution. Needs transparency without requiring daily involvement. |

---

### D7 — Status & Distinction (SCARF: Status + Magids Motivator)
**What it captures:** The desire to be recognized as ahead of the curve, smarter than peers, or making a bold/differentiated call.

**Probing question:** "Is this buyer a fast-follower (wants what works) or an early adopter (wants to be first and be seen as such)?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| Early adopter. If they can say "we automated GTM while competitors are still hiring BDRs" at a YC dinner, that's a status win. Messaging should frame adoption as a signal of sophistication, not a shortcut. | Early adopter in sourcing tech. If a GP can say "we see deals two weeks before the market because of how we've built our sourcing stack," that is a differentiator they will repeat to LPs. |

---

### D8 — Success Criteria / ROI Defensibility (Revella Ring 2 + Apollo)
**What it captures:** The specific, measurable outcomes they expect — AND the numbers they need to defend the decision internally or to a board.

**Probing question:** "What specific metric must move, by how much, in what timeframe, for this person to call this a win?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| 3–5 qualified demo calls per week within 60 days. Any outcome short of that is a sunk cost, not a learning. Secondary: reply rate above 15% on cold outreach. | 3–4 proprietary deals per month in target sectors that were not in any intermediary's pipeline. Must see pattern within 90 days or the sourcing hypothesis is wrong. |

---

### D9 — Perceived Barriers (Revella Ring 3 + JTBD Anxiety)
**What it captures:** The specific objections, fears, and skepticisms that will kill the deal before it closes — especially the ones the buyer won't say out loud.

**Probing question:** "What is the most likely reason this buyer dismisses this offer in the first 10 seconds, and what is the silent fear underneath it?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| "I've tried Apollo/Instantly/Clay — it just spammed people and hurt my domain." Silent fear: "AI outreach will make me look desperate and generic to the exact people I need to impress." | "We already have a sourcing team and a network." Silent fear: "If we admit we have a sourcing problem, it signals to the market that our deal flow is weak — and LPs will ask about it." |

---

### D10 — Insight Value (Challenger / Commercial Insight)
**What it captures:** The desire to receive a genuinely new framing of a problem — to learn something about their own business from the outreach itself.

**Probing question:** "Is there a counter-intuitive truth about their industry or situation that would make them re-examine their current approach?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| "The best technical founders are losing to mediocre products with better distribution — not because they lack skills, but because they're spending time on the wrong layer of the stack." (Reframes outreach as an engineering problem, not a sales problem.) | "The funds that consistently outperform aren't winning at picking — they're winning at seeing deals first, through founder networks, before those deals are visible on AngelList. Sourcing compounds; picking doesn't." (Reframes sourcing as a compounding asset, not a quarterly metric. Note: framed as a thesis, not a statistic — do not assert unverifiable performance data in outreach copy.) |

---

### D11 — Temporal Urgency (Trigger Events + JTBD Push Accumulation)
**What it captures:** The time-sensitivity of the buyer's situation — whether the pain has a natural deadline or competitive forcing function.

**Probing question:** "Is there a specific event, deadline, or competitive pressure that makes solving this more urgent this quarter than next?"

| ICP-A (Developer Founder) | ICP-B (VC Firm) |
|---|---|
| Runway is finite. If they haven't hit pipeline-producing GTM in the next 90 days, the Series A conversation shifts from traction to survival. Board pressure is building. | Fund deployment cycle. Many mid-size VCs are in Q3–Q4 of their current fund and need to show LPs a differentiated pipeline story before the next fund raise in 12–18 months. |

## Part 4: Dimension Priority by ICP

| Dimension | ICP-A Priority | ICP-B Priority |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Trigger Pain | High — recent growth wall | High — missed deal |
| D2 Functional Job | High | High |
| D3 Emotional Job | **Highest** — identity as builder | High |
| D4 Social/Identity Job | High — technical founder tribe | **Highest** — VC brand positioning |
| D5 Career Risk | High — co-founder/investor optics | High — LP trust |
| D6 Autonomy | **Highest** — technical control need | Moderate |
| D7 Status/Distinction | High | High |
| D8 Success Criteria | High — needs measurable ROI | High — needs deal-level proof |
| D9 Perceived Barriers | **Highest** — prior tool failures | **Highest** — won't admit sourcing gap |
| D10 Insight Value | High — reframe as engineering problem | High — reframe as compounding asset |
| D11 Temporal Urgency | High — runway pressure | High — fund cycle |

## Part 5: How the Agent Uses This Model

**Research phase (before writing a single line):** For a given prospect, the agent queries public signals — LinkedIn activity, company blog, job postings, press releases, funding announcements, competitor moves — and maps each dimension. Not every dimension will be answerable. The agent notes which are strong, which are unknown, and which are likely suppressed (the ones the buyer won't say out loud but which drive the decision).

**Message selection:** Lead with the 1–2 highest-evidenced dimensions. Anchor on D1 (Trigger), then move to D3 (Emotional) or D4 (Social). Address D9 (Perceived Barrier) either directly or by implication. Never lead with D2 (Functional) alone — that is feature pitching, not desire matching.

**The anti-pattern to avoid:** Most B2B outbound messages address only D2 (functional) and D8 (ROI). This model ensures the agent captures D3, D4, D5, D6, D9, and D10 — the dimensions that separate resonant messages from polished noise.
