## 1. The Value Equation

**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice).** The hand-drawn equation appears in Hormozi's own [Pricing & Value checklist PDF](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Pricing-Value-Checklist.pdf) with the four operational moves verbatim:

- **Maximize Dream Outcome** — "solve problem worth solving"
- **Maximize Perceived Likelihood of Success** — "testimonials & proven case studies"
- **Minimize Time to Success** — "How can we make this faster? How can we show progress?"
- **Minimize Effort & Sacrifice** — "How can we make this easier? More Seamless? Convenient?"

Two of his own glosses, from a [long-form interview](https://youngandprofiting.com/alex-hormozi-the-value-equation-how-to-make-offers-so-good-people-feel-stupid-saying-no-e199/) (his words, transcribed):

- **The denominator is the moat:** "The businesses that are worth a fortune, they spent all of their time on the bottom side because the bottom side is usually the competitive moat." Dream-outcome claims are easy to copy; speed and effort-removal require operations.
- **Value is perceived value only:** "if we do not communicate it, they will not perceive it and they will not value it, which means you don't get paid for it." An offer improvement invisible in the copy is, commercially, not an improvement — this is the joint between gtme-offer and gtme-write.

**Epistemics, stated honestly:** this is a practitioner framework, not peer-reviewed theory. The variables are not independently measurable, the multiplication/division structure is rhetorical (nobody computes a value score), and the supporting numbers on his own book page ("$36 for every $1 spent," "over $120,000,000 across four different industries" — [shop.acquisition.com](https://shop.acquisition.com/products/100m-offers-hardcover)) are self-reported marketing claims. What the equation earns its place with: it's a *complete checklist of the four levers* — every corpus copy doctrine maps onto one of them, which is why it works as the WHAT-layer spine.

**Corpus reconciliation:**

| Value-equation lever | Corpus counterpart | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | `04` dream-outcome/gain framing; coldemailchris framework 3 "{Mechanism} guarantees {Dream Result} in {Timeframe}" ([`11` §3.4](11-x-primary-sources.md)) | CONFIRMS |
| Perceived likelihood | `04` social proof; `11` §2.6 #9 "proof to THIS reader"; verifiable-proof resolution ([`11` §9](11-x-primary-sources.md)) | CONFIRMS |
| Time delay | coldemailchris front-end starters all carry a clock — "24 hrs", "ready to paste" ([`11` §3.7](11-x-primary-sources.md)) | CONFIRMS |
| Effort & sacrifice | `04` §4.8 low-friction CTA doctrine; `11` §2.5 zero pre-engagement demands | CONFIRMS |

**NEW to the corpus:** the corpus had all four levers scattered as copy rules; nothing framed them as properties of the *offer itself*, to be engineered before any copy is written. That reframe — fix the offer, then write — is the reason gtme-offer sits upstream of gtme-write.

## 2. Offer Construction: Problems → Solutions → Trim & Stack

### 2.1 Hormozi's process (primary: [Offer Creation checklist PDF](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Offer%2BCreation%2BChecklist.pdf))

The PDF's own five-step sketch: ① Dream outcome? → ② Problems/obstacles → ③ "List ALL problems as solutions" → ④ Maximize profit/value → ⑤ "Final high value high profit offer stack." Expanded:

1. **Define their dream outcome.**
2. **List out every possible problem they can encounter** — the checklist forces exhaustiveness: "every intricate step," "everything that happens before and after achieving their goals," "what *next* problem will they face." Each problem is probed on the four value-equation axes: worth it? easier? faster? "enjoyable, sustainable, and something they believe *they* can do?"
3. **List every possible solution to the problems** — one solution per problem, phrased as the obstacle inverted ("How to [get outcome] without [pain] even if [greatest obstacle]").
4. **Run solutions through the Delivery Cube** — six dimensions for *how* each solution ships: group ratio (1-1 / small group / one-to-many); effort on their part (DIY / DWY / DFY); support level (SMS→Zoom, expert vs normal); consumption type (live, recorded, in person, written, audio, video); speed & convenience (24/7 vs 9-5, reply-speed tiers); and the **10x / 1/10th test**: "What would I deliver if my product cost 10x its current amount" and "if my product cost 1/10th its current cost and had to provide *more* value than I currently am at my current price?"
5. **Trim & stack** — score every delivery vehicle on a value×cost 2×2. His definitions, verbatim: high value = "meaningful, fast, easy, and believe they can do it and enjoy it" (the value equation again); high cost = "requires people to scale"; low cost = "can scale without people but with up front effort instead." Rules: "Remove all items that are NOT high value"; "Keep only a handful of high cost high value, and everything else should be high value low cost"; and — notable for an early-stage motion — "Start with something that is less scalable (more high cost high value) to get people to say yes and begin monetization."

The same PDF carries the thesis line, hand-highlighted: **"Make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no."**

### 2.2 Beyond Hormozi: the same shape from B2B operators

[Pierre Hérubel's B2B offer-creation guide](https://pierreherubel.substack.com/p/b2b-offer-creation-complete-guide) independently converges on the structure, with two additions Hormozi lacks:

- **The PURE problem gate** — the problem an offer targets must be **P**ainful, **U**rgent, **R**ecognized, **E**xpensive. This is a *pre-condition on step 2*: problems failing PURE don't earn offer components. It maps almost 1:1 onto the corpus's **PQS fields** (trigger/impact/urgency/message-angle, [`11` §3.7](11-x-primary-sources.md)) — PQS is PURE per segment. **CONFIRMS**, and gives gtme-icp→gtme-offer a shared vocabulary.
- **The offer ladder** — front-end offer ("simple, low commitment entry point," fast result inside a month) → mid-tier → backend high-ticket. His 7 ranked success factors (PURE problem, market demand, proprietary differentiation, low friction, fast time-to-value, message clarity, packaging) restate the value equation's denominators at ranks 4–5 — independent convergence worth noting.

**The synthesis the suite needs (NEW):** the corpus's four **front-end offer starters** (audit / playbook / teardown / benchmark, [`11` §3.7](11-x-primary-sources.md)) are not a separate doctrine — they are the *outbound-sized slice of the grand slam offer*: the trimmed-and-stacked offer's cheapest high-value-low-cost component, detached and given away as touch-1 proof. gtme-offer should emit both artifacts from one construction: the full stack (for the close) and the front-end slice (for gtme-write's WHAT layer).

## 3. Guarantees & Risk Reversal

Primary: [Unbeatable Guarantee checklist PDF](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Unbeatable-Guarantee-Checklist.pdf). The framing sentence, hand-drawn on the sheet: **"If you don't achieve X in Y time, we will…"** The taxonomy:

1. **Unconditional** — no questions asked. Sales framing verbatim: "I'm not asking you to say yes or no today, I'm asking you to make an informed decision."
2. **Conditional** — "should always be 'better than money back'," with the load-bearing design rule: **"Always match the guarantee terms with the activation points in your program — what does someone actually have to do to be successful, make those the terms."** Ten named variants on the sheet: outsized refund (2-3x), service guarantee (keep working free), modified service (double the time free), credit-based, personal 1-1 service, hotel & airfare, wage payment, release of commitment, delayed second payment, first-outcome guarantee (pay their ancillary costs until first outcome).
3. **Anti-guarantee** — "All sales are final, best for exploding opportunity and high level services."
4. **Implied** — performance/revshare/profit-share: only paid on results ($X per sale, 10% of top-line, X% of profit).
5. **Stacking** — conditional+unconditional together; two conditionals on small & big outcomes; or same terms at short & long timelines.

His pricing claim (practitioner claim, [interview](https://youngandprofiting.com/alex-hormozi-the-value-equation-how-to-make-offers-so-good-people-feel-stupid-saying-no-e199/)): "If you add a guarantee, you can, in a very real way, increase your price because... you have decreased their risk." A secondary summary of the book adds that service-based guarantees outperform money-back for higher-ticket offers ([Faxon summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary) — book paraphrase, not independent data).

**Corpus reconciliation:**

- **CONFIRMS + upgrades [`11` §2.1](11-x-primary-sources.md):** the praised Nick Abraham specimen's "you don't pay until you've hired" is a *delayed-payment conditional guarantee* — the corpus filed risk reversal as a NEW copy element with no menu behind it. This taxonomy is the menu; gtme-offer picks the guarantee, gtme-write only phrases it.
- **Mechanism grounding via [`04` loss aversion](04-psychology-nepq-persuasion.md):** prospect theory (λ≈1.5–2.5, `04`'s sourced range) says a potential loss weighs roughly twice an equivalent gain. A guarantee doesn't add upside — it deletes the buyer's loss branch and moves it to the seller, which is why risk reversal punches above its word count. Same caveat inherits: the effect magnitude is contested; treat "~2×" as a rough central estimate.
- **Offer-integrity resolution ([`11` §9](11-x-primary-sources.md) intra-source tension — "don't write checks the ops can't cash"):** Hormozi's activation-point rule is the honest version. A conditional guarantee whose terms = the client actions fulfillment actually needs is a check ops *can* cash; an unconditional guarantee on a service with heavy fulfillment cost is the one that bankrupts (his own warning per the book summaries). The human gate (§6) checks exactly this.

## 4. Naming, Bonuses, Scarcity & Urgency

### 4.1 Naming — the MAGIC formula (primary: [Product Naming checklist PDF](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Product-Naming-Checklist.pdf))

**M**ake a magnetic reason why · **A**nnounce your avatar · **G**ive them a goal · **I**ndicate a time interval · **C**omplete with a container word. ("Spring 28-Day Blueprint," "Free 6Wk Lean Challenge" — his sketch: "same bundle, different wrapper.") When an offer fatigues, refresh **in reverse order of difficulty and operational drag**: creative → body copy → headline/wrapper → duration → enhancer (free/discount component) → monetization structure. Rename before you rebuild.

**Corpus note:** MAGIC is a *landing-page/ad* naming formula. It does not override cold-email subject doctrine — subjects stay 1–3 words, lowercase, internal-mimic ([`11` §3.7/§2.1](11-x-primary-sources.md)). MAGIC applies to the offer's *name* as it appears in the body, on the front-end asset, and anywhere gtme-publish ships it. No conflict; different surfaces.

### 4.2 Bonuses (primary: [Bonus Creation checklist PDF](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Bonus-Creation-Checklist.pdf))

Add 1–10 bonuses — "the sizzle." Rules verbatim: bonuses "should be more valuable than core thing"; "present after price is said"; **"stack bonuses instead of discounts to get them to say yes"** (price integrity — the discount reflex destroys the premium-price leg of the offer); "bonuses should solve all perceived problems"; always price-tag and justify each; **"tools & checklists are better than additional trainings (as the effort & time are lower... the value equation still reigns supreme)"**; solve "their next problem before they even encounter it"; and a 4-level ladder of other-people's-products (free/discounted → +affiliate commissions → +exclusive features/licenses).

**Corpus fit:** bonuses barely exist in a ≤75-word cold email — but they exist in the *offer document, proposal, and close*. The corpus's C4 caveat holds ([`11` §9](11-x-primary-sources.md)): incentives scale to the target's hourly value. And "tools > trainings" is the same physics as `11` §8.6's demo-in-disguise lead magnet: lowest-effort proof wins.

### 4.3 Scarcity & urgency — and the ethics rule (primary: [Scarcity & Urgency checklist PDF](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Scarcity-Urgency-Checklist.pdf))

His entire published menu is structural, not theatrical. Scarcity (function of quantity): only accepting X clients — total, per week, or per class/cohort. Urgency (function of time): cohort-based ("next group starts next week"), rolling seasonal, pricing/bonus-based ("these bonuses are going away"), exploding opportunity. Book-level principle: "When demand increases, cut supply" ([Faxon summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary), book paraphrase).

**Ethics rule, consistent with the corpus:** every mechanism on his sheet is *checkable* — a real cap, a real start date, a real expiring bonus. The corpus rule stands unchanged and gates all of them: manufactured urgency and fake scarcity backfire — "it triggers fairness and trust violations that permanently damage credibility" ([`04`](04-psychology-nepq-persuasion.md), loss-aversion caveat), and fake scarcity is a documented anti-specimen failure mode ([`11` §2.2](11-x-primary-sources.md), levelsio's "3 business owners" catch). **Operationalization for gtme-offer: a scarcity/urgency element may only enter the offer if it corresponds to a verifiable operational fact (capacity cap, cohort date, expiring bonus that actually expires). If capacity is genuinely unlimited, the offer carries no scarcity line.** For a solo GTM engineer the true cap is usually real and small — client slots per month — which makes honest scarcity cheap to claim.

## 5. Offer Tiers & Volume Math

The corpus's only offer-conditioned conversion prior is coldemailchris's tier table ([`11` §3.1](11-x-primary-sources.md)): incredible offer = 25–200 contacts per lead; good = 200–500; decent = 500–1,000; bad/commodity = 1,000–10,000. This pass looked for independent corroboration or conflict.

**Finding: the industry benchmark literature doesn't measure the variable at all.** Two current benchmark roundups were fetched and checked: [InboxKit's 2026 reply-rate benchmarks](https://www.inboxkit.com/learn/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks-2026) (Woodpecker 1–5% on 20M+ emails, Lemlist 3.1% on 50M+, SmartLead top-quartile 5–8%) and [Whali's response-rate benchmarks](https://whali.co.uk/blog/cold-email-response-rates-benchmarks) (3.4% average, top performers >10%, big spreads by industry and role). Both condition on personalization, infrastructure, follow-up count, and length — **neither conditions on offer quality anywhere**. InboxKit explicitly quantifies personalization (1–2% generic vs 8–15% high-personalization) and infrastructure (+8–16pts) but has no offer axis; Whali likewise.

So the reconciliation is:

- **No CONFLICT found** — nothing fetched contradicts the tier table.
- **No independent corroboration either** — the table remains single-source (an operator's claimed 5M+ send history, [`11` §3](11-x-primary-sources.md)). Treat the *ordering* as robust (it's the value equation applied to markets: commodity offers score near-zero on differentiation and perceived likelihood) and the *specific bands* as one practitioner's calibration.
- **Inference, flagged as inference:** the enormous unexplained spread in published benchmarks (sub-1% to >10% "average" reply rates across sources measuring the same channel) is consistent with offer quality being a large unmeasured confounder. Benchmark reports sell sending tools; the offer isn't their product, so they don't instrument it.

**Volume math composition (NEW):** tier table × the corpus's P(≥1 sale) = 1 − (1−p)^N formula ([`11` §8.3](11-x-primary-sources.md)) gives gtme-list its plan: the human-gated offer tier sets expected contacts-per-lead, which sets N for a target lead count, which sets list size, inbox count, and timeline *before* any sending. A tier-4 offer demanding 5,000+ contacts per lead is also a signal to go back to gtme-offer rather than scale infrastructure — fix the offer before buying domains. This is the diagnostic branch gtme-measure already reserved (channel / step / **offer**, [`11` §10](11-x-primary-sources.md)).

## 6. The Gate: Grand-Slam Test Operationalized

The bar, from the book itself (quoted via [summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary); tagline confirmed on [Hormozi's own product page](https://shop.acquisition.com/products/100m-offers-hardcover)): a Grand Slam Offer is *"an offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available, combining an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee with a money model (payment terms) that allows you to get paid to get new customers."* And the test: *"You don't have to be the best. You just need to make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no."*

"Feel stupid saying no" is not directly checkable; the components are. Hormozi's own checklist sequence (the roman numerals running III→XI across his PDFs: pricing → value → components → trim & stack → scarcity → urgency → bonuses → guarantees → naming) *is* his operationalization. Compressed with the PURE gate (§2.2) into the checklist a human reviews — one yes/no per line, any NO blocks the pipeline:

| # | Gate question | Fails when | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PURE problem** — is the target pain painful, urgent, recognized, expensive for this PQS? | Offer built on a pain the ICP doesn't know it has | §2.2; [`11` §3.7](11-x-primary-sources.md) |
| 2 | **Incomparability** — can the prospect get a like-for-like quote from a competitor? | Commodity framing ("we do SEO") → price shopping | §6 definition; §2.2 proprietary positioning |
| 3 | **Value equation, all four levers** — does the offer explicitly raise dream outcome + likelihood and cut time + effort? | Any lever unaddressed (usually the denominators) | §1 |
| 4 | **Every named problem has a component** — does the stack answer the full §2 problem list? | "You must resolve every obstacle a buyer believes they will have to convert the highest amount of people" (book, via [summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary)) | §2.1 |
| 5 | **Trim check** — is every component high-value, and all but a handful low-cost? | Fulfillment-heavy stack that can't survive success | §2.1 step 5 |
| 6 | **Guarantee ops can cash** — conditional terms = real activation points; worst-case payout survivable? | Copy promises the inventory doesn't back — the [`11` §9](11-x-primary-sources.md) offer-integrity failure | §3 |
| 7 | **Scarcity/urgency true** — does every scarcity/urgency line map to a verifiable operational fact? | Fake caps/deadlines — permanent trust damage ([`04`](04-psychology-nepq-persuasion.md)) | §4.3 |
| 8 | **Premium price held** — priced above market with bonuses instead of discounts? | Discount reflex; price no longer signals value | §4.2; [Pricing PDF](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/Offer%20Checklists%20-%20PDF%20Downloads/Pricing-Value-Checklist.pdf) ("be more expensive than everyone else") |
| 9 | **Named + front-end slice cut** — MAGIC-named, and a touch-1 front-end offer (audit/playbook/teardown/benchmark shape) extracted for gtme-write? | Full offer with no cold-outreach-sized entry point | §4.1, §2.2 |
| 10 | **Honest tier estimate** — human assigns a `11` §3.1 tier; tier 4 → return to construction, don't proceed to volume | Volume planning on a commodity offer | §5 |

**Why a human gate and not a rubric score:** questions 2, 6, 7, and 10 require knowledge the agent doesn't have — competitive substitutes, fulfillment capacity, operational truth, and market taste. This is the same shape as gtme-score's `effort_mode` fork ([`11` §5.3](11-x-primary-sources.md)): judgment stays human, the agent prepares the judgment surface. The per-*email* gate (the 5-questions test, [`11` §3.3](11-x-primary-sources.md)) then inherits: an email can only answer "how will you make me money" as well as the offer behind it allows — gate the offer once, and every downstream email starts from a passing answer.
